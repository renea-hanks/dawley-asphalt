What to Expect

How Much Does It Cost to Have an Asphalt Driveway Put In?

This is the question every homeowner asks — and the honest answer is that asphalt driveway installation cannot be accurately priced without a site assessment. A number given without seeing the property is a guess, not a quote. Here is why.

The cost of a new asphalt driveway is determined by a specific set of variables, each of which must be evaluated on-site before any figure is meaningful:

  • Square FootageThe total paved area is the starting point. Length, width, and any additional aprons, turnarounds, or extensions all factor into material and labor requirements.
  • Existing Surface ConditionIs there an existing driveway to be removed? If so, the old material must be excavated and hauled. The condition and depth of the existing base determines how much subbase preparation is required before new asphalt can be laid.
  • Subbase RequirementsColorado's freeze-thaw cycles demand a properly prepared and compacted subbase. If the native soil is unstable, expansive, or water-retaining, additional base material is required. Cutting this step produces a surface that fails early — regardless of the quality of the asphalt above it.
  • Grade and DrainageWater is asphalt's primary adversary. Every driveway we install is engineered to shed water away from the surface and away from the structure it serves. If the existing grade directs water toward the garage or house foundation, correcting it adds to the scope — but failing to correct it guarantees premature failure.
  • Asphalt Thickness and MixResidential driveways in Colorado Springs are typically installed at 3 to 4 inches of compacted asphalt over a prepared aggregate base. Driveways that will carry heavier loads — RVs, equipment, frequent delivery trucks — require greater thickness and adjusted mix specification.
  • Access and Site ConditionsSteep grades, tight access for equipment, or proximity to structures, landscaping, or utilities all affect the complexity and cost of the work.

What we can tell you is this: a properly installed asphalt driveway in Colorado Springs, done to the standard this climate demands, is an investment that should last 20 to 30 years with appropriate maintenance. A driveway installed without proper subbase preparation, drainage engineering, or correct mix specification will not come close to that lifespan — regardless of its initial price.

The complimentary consultation exists for exactly this reason. We walk the site, evaluate every variable, and give you a precise number — not an estimate built on assumptions.

How It's Done

The Asphalt Driveway Installation Process

01

Site Assessment and Consultation

Before any work begins, we walk the property. We evaluate the existing surface, subbase condition, grade, drainage patterns, and access. This is where the scope is determined and the specification is set. Nothing is assumed.

02

Demolition and Removal

If an existing surface is present — concrete, old asphalt, or gravel — it is removed and hauled from the site. The area is cleared to native soil or existing aggregate base, depending on its condition.

03

Subbase Preparation and Grading

This is the most critical phase of the installation. The subbase is graded to the engineered slope — directing water away from the structure and toward appropriate drainage. It is compacted to the density required to carry the anticipated load without settling. In Colorado Springs, this step must account for freeze-thaw cycling. A subbase that allows water infiltration will heave, settle, and fail.

04

Aggregate Base Installation

A compacted aggregate base is installed over the prepared subgrade. This layer provides the structural support that the asphalt surface distributes load to. Its depth and composition are specified for the soil conditions and anticipated use.

05

Asphalt Installation

Hot mix asphalt is delivered and laid to the specified depth. The mix is selected for Colorado's climate — binder grade, aggregate size, and gradation are all specified for freeze-thaw performance and load requirements. The material is spread and screeded to a uniform depth, then compacted with a roller to achieve the required density. Compaction is not optional — it is what closes the voids that water would otherwise exploit.

06

Edging and Finishing

Edges are finished to a clean, stable line. Transitions to existing concrete, aprons, curbs, or garage slabs are properly addressed. The surface is checked for grade compliance — water must sheet off the finished surface to the intended drainage points.

07

Cure Period and Maintenance Guidance

New asphalt requires a cure period before it reaches full hardness. We provide specific guidance on when the surface can receive foot traffic, vehicle traffic, and heavy loads. We also establish the maintenance timeline — when the first sealcoat is appropriate and what to watch for in the first seasons of the surface's life.
